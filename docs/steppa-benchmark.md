# Steppa benchmark

Last checked: September 1, 2026

This is a product benchmark for StridePool, not a brand or content copy source. The implementation intentionally uses a different name, visual identity, tone, and product emphasis.

## Direct competitor: Steppa — Step Challenges

### Positioning

Steppa frames itself as a skill-based commitment device: put a fixed stake on a daily step goal, finish every day in the challenge window, and split a shared prize pool with the other finishers. Its core message is “win real cash by walking,” with the argument that the outcome depends on the participant’s own behavior rather than luck.

### Observed product loop

1. Pick a challenge with a daily goal and fixed duration.
2. Pay an entry fee/stake.
3. Steppa reads steps from Apple Health; manually entered steps do not count.
4. Complete the goal every day.
5. Wallet balance is credited when the challenge settles.
6. Withdraw to PayPal or a US bank account, or roll the balance into another challenge.

### Product surface and mechanics

- Challenge formats are described as daily, weekly, and multi-day.
- A public challenge example shows a named challenge, date range, daily step goal, prize pool, and current participant count.
- Prize pool is based on total entries; finishers share the pool.
- The website says the pool pays out 100% to winners, with a small processing fee shown before confirmation and a creator fee for public challenges.
- The App Store listing references Stripe payments, a wallet, earnings history, progress charts, real-time updates, friend/community play, and clear qualification requirements.
- Paid challenges are restricted to adults and availability depends on location.
- The App Store listing carries an 18+ rating and a frequent contests / gambling descriptor.
- Third-party wearable steps can count when written into Apple Health, but the product warns that sync reliability varies.

### Public traction signals

The homepage currently claims 4B+ total steps, 80k+ players, 20k+ challenges completed, and $800k+ paid out. The same site displays an App Store rating around 4.5 stars with roughly 1.4k ratings. These are self-reported marketing metrics and should be treated as directional rather than independently verified.

### User feedback signals

Public review snippets include praise for motivation, legitimate payouts, and responsive support when tracking problems occur. A repeated concern is that the percentage taken by the app is not sufficiently transparent. This is the most actionable trust gap to address in StridePool.

## Adjacent alternatives

| Product | Core job | What it teaches us |
| --- | --- | --- |
| StepBet | Personalized step goals backed by money | Personalization and goal-setting are a strong alternative to one-size-fits-all contests. |
| StepUp | Free step challenges for friends, family, workplaces, and communities | Social and group play can reach people who do not want financial stakes. |
| Sweatcoin | Step count converted into an in-app currency | Rewards do not have to be winner-take-all or cash-only. |
| Paace | Walk to earn points, rewards, leagues, and challenges | Ongoing reward loops can complement short challenge windows. |
| Stepracers | Real-time races with custom goals and leaderboards | Competitive energy can exist without a shared cash pool. |
| Do nothing / personal tracker | Walk without a commitment | StridePool must reduce setup friction and make the social payoff obvious quickly. |

## White space for StridePool

1. **Show the math before the ask.** Display entry fee, platform fee, current pool, estimated payout range, and the exact condition for eligibility in a single readable breakdown.
2. **Make social accountability first-class.** Support teams, invite links, private circles, and encouraging activity updates instead of only a global leaderboard.
3. **Design for more than iPhone.** Start with a browser-friendly demo and plan for Apple Health plus Android Health Connect when production integrations are added.
4. **Offer non-cash ways to participate.** Free community challenges and sponsor-funded rewards widen the funnel and reduce the gambling-adjacent feel.
5. **Handle real life better.** Add grace days, injury pause requests, and clear support/review paths rather than making users feel they must choose between walking sick or losing everything.
6. **Make the product feel trustworthy.** Explain verification, settlement timing, fees, eligibility, and privacy in plain language before asking for money or health permissions.
